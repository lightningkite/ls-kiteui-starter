Order model — three viable shapes

Two axes generate four shapes; one is impractical, leaving three credible options. The mockup walks Combined Basket; this page surfaces Separated Procurement and describes Flat Order for comparison.

The two axes

  • Axis A — Durable Prescription entity? YES: Rx is a long-lived clinical authorization, signed once, filled many times. NO: each fill captures its own clinical details (no shared parent).
  • Axis B — Separate dispatch entity? YES: approved orders queue for a procurement operator who picks pharmacy and creates a PharmacyOrder later. NO: pharmacy is picked at order entry; approval-then-dispatch happens in one motion.

Axis A = NO + Axis B = YES is impractical (deferred dispatch without a durable Rx makes the procurement queue's identity strange), so three shapes remain.

Side-by-side

Combined Basket

Current mockup
[ Patient + ship-to ] [ Add Rx line · Rx line ] [ Eligible pharmacy ▣ ] [ Submit (1 ID.me) ] ↓ dispatch

Axis A: durable Prescription. Axis B: dispatch combined with approval.

MA composes the basket: patient → ship-to → Rx lines (existing or new) → pharmacy → submit. One screen, one motion. Pharmacy eligibility is computed from the basket.

Best when: most shipments go direct-to-patient or to one clinic at a time; pharmacy choice is largely automatic given lowest-price-that-covers-the-basket.

Separated Procurement

Alternative
[ Patient + Rx line ] [ Approve (1 ID.me) ] ↓ [ Procurement Queue ] [ Group · pick pharm ] [ Confirm → dispatch ]

Axis A: durable Prescription. Axis B: dispatch deferred to procurement.

Prescribers approve individual PrescriptionOrders without picking pharmacy. ClinicAdmin (or a dedicated role) opens the Procurement Queue, groups by destination, picks pharmacy(ies), and confirms — that creates the PharmacyOrder and triggers dispatch.

Best when: clinic-bulk-shipping is common — many patients' Rxs ship together to the clinic for in-clinic distribution. Procurement role enables packing optimization.

Flat Order (PRD § 09)

Not recommended
[ Order ] [ OrderItem ] [ OrderItem ] [ Submit ] ↓ dispatch

Axis A: no durable Prescription. Axis B: dispatch combined.

PRD § 09's literal shape: Order + OrderItem. Each fill captures its own product/strength/sig; refills are new orders with duplicated fields. No shared parent record.

Trade-off: Slightly less code in the short term, but refill cleanliness and clinical-record permanence both suffer — every refill is a clone with no canonical authorization to point at, complicating DEA audit responses and clinical chart consistency.

Lightning Kite recommendation

Axis A = YES (durable Prescription) always. Both Combined Basket and Separated Procurement carry it; Flat Order's omission is the main reason we don't recommend it.

Axis B (separated vs combined dispatch) is conditional on Gameday's ops:

  • Common clinic-bulk-shipping (≥5 Rxs/day per clinic going to the clinic address rather than patient addresses): Separated Procurement. The procurement role pays for itself.
  • Mostly direct-to-patient or low daily volume: Combined Basket. Avoids an extra screen and extra role.

Question for Jon: at a typical Gameday clinic, what percentage of prescriptions ship to the clinic for in-clinic distribution vs. directly to the patient's home? The answer picks the shape.
